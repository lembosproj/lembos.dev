## Users

People join by **invitation**, or by signing in with GitHub if
[the integration](/docs/install/github) is on. Nobody registers themselves.

<Screenshot name="users" ratio={2.9} alt="The users: each person's name, email address and whether they are invited or active." />

## Groups

A **group** is a team, a department or a project. Groups nest, and they **own** everything in Lembos:
every system, component, blueprint, environment and policy has an owning group.

Membership is how people reach things. A member of a group reaches what that group owns, and what every
group beneath it owns.

<Screenshot name="groups" ratio={2.6} alt="The groups: squads, tribes and the engineering organisation, each with its parent and number of members." />

## Roles

A **role** is a named set of permissions: *may this person do this kind of thing at all?* Lembos ships
three roles for people:

| Role              | For                                                                                   |
|-------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Contributor**   | Product teams: describe software in the catalog, request runs, assemble and promote releases |
| **Maintainer**    | Platform teams: blueprints, execution targets, environments, placement rules and policies — and approving other people's runs |
| **Administrator** | Everything a person can do, including managing users, groups, roles and service accounts |

You can create your own roles. See [Built-in roles](/docs/reference/roles) for details.

<Screenshot name="roles" ratio={2.6} alt="The roles: the built-in Administrator, Contributor and Maintainer, and the machine-only roles, each with its permission count." />

Roles and ownership work together: a Contributor can deploy only what their groups own. And
[policies](/docs/use/governance) add the last check — whether this particular change is allowed now.

## Service accounts

A **service account** is a machine: a CI pipeline, an integration, an agent. It holds roles like a person,
but is never a member of a group. Instead it is granted reach over named group subtrees explicitly, so it
does not change when teams are reorganised.

When a service account acts on a person's behalf, it can do only what *both* of them may do.

<Screenshot name="service-accounts" ratio={2.8} alt="The service accounts: the platform's own agents, each with what it does, whether it acts on its own or only for a person, its reach and its status." />

Maintainers and Administrators can see each service account's roles, effective permissions and reach.
