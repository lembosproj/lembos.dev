title: Notifications
description: What waits on your decision, and what failed in what you answer for.

Lembos tells you when something waits on you, and when something you answer for fails:

| You are told when                       | Who is told                                                                   |
|-----------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| A run waits for approval                | Whoever could approve it — never the person who asked for it                  |
| A release waits for approval to move on | Whoever could approve it                                                      |
| A run or a release fails                | Whoever started it, and whoever approves runs for the team                    |
| A check fails                           | Whoever may change what failed: the execution target, credential or blueprint |

**Who** is decided by your groups and roles, not by subscribing. A notification goes to the people in the group that
owns what it is about; when nobody there can act, to the group above it, and so on up. Join a team with the right
role and you are told from its next event on.

## Your inbox

The **bell** beside your account in the Portal counts what you have not read, and updates as things happen. Open it
for the latest few, or **See all notifications** for the full list — which you can narrow to what needs you, to what
is unread, or to one topic. Your home page starts with the same: what waits on your decision and what you have not
read.

A notification opens on its **details** first, beside the list: what happened, whether it has been answered — and
how — and why it reached you. **Open the run** (or the release, or the credential) takes you where it is answered.
Opening a notification marks it read. When somebody else answers it first — approves the run, or the check
recovers — it stays in your list, saying how it ended, and no longer counts on the bell.

## Settings

The **Settings** tab says where each topic reaches you. Every notification reaches your inbox; it is the record
everything else adds to.

### Your channels

When your installation's mail relay offers notifications, an **Email** column sits beside the inbox: tick a topic and
it is emailed to your account's address too. Until you choose, email carries what waits on your decision — run and
release approvals — so an approval reaches you even away from the Portal. Each email leads to the notification's
details in your inbox.

Email goes only to people whose account is active: once somebody leaves, nothing more is sent to them.

When your installation has the Lembos **Slack** app, a **Slack** column sits beside Email. Connect it first:

1. **Connect your Slack**, above the table, and give your Slack member id — on your Slack profile, under ⋮, **Copy
   member ID**.
2. The Lembos app messages you a **code** in Slack. Enter it from the same place (**Enter the code**); it works for a
   day.
3. Once connected, tick the topics Slack messages you about. **Disconnect** stops it; **Change member id** moves it.

When your installation has the Lembos **Teams** app, a **Teams** column sits beside them. Connect it the same way:

1. **Connect your Teams**, above the table. Your Lembos email is filled in; change it if Teams knows you by another.
   Lembos looks you up in your organisation's directory by it.
2. The Lembos bot sends you a **code** in a Teams chat. Enter it from the same place (**Enter the code**); it works for
   a day. If nothing arrives, the Lembos app is not installed for you yet: ask your Teams administrator.
3. Once connected, tick the topics Teams messages you about. **Disconnect** stops it; changing the email moves it.

A Slack or Teams message that asked for a decision is updated once it is answered — "Approved", "Recovered" — so it
never keeps asking. Each leads to the notification in Lembos with **Open in Lembos**; nothing is answered from inside
Slack or Teams.

### Your groups

Below your own channels is every group you belong to, with the addresses its notifications are sent to besides its
members' own — a team mailing list, a Slack channel or a Teams channel. A group's address is sent each notification
**once**, for the team, rather than once per member.

Anybody who can change the group can manage its addresses:

1. **Add email**, **Add Slack channel** or **Add Teams channel** beside the group, enter where it goes, and choose its
   topics. By default it also carries what the group's subgroups own.
   - **Email**: the address.
   - **Slack**: the channel's id, at the bottom of its details in Slack. A private channel has to invite the Lembos app
     first; a channel the app cannot see is refused as you add it.
   - **Teams**: the channel's link — in Teams, the channel's **⋯** menu, **Get link to channel**, **Copy**. The Lembos
     app has to be in the team first: a team owner adds it. Private channels cannot be used, because Teams does not let
     a bot post in them.
2. A **code** is sent to the address, or posted in the channel — in Teams, as a new post. Nothing else goes there
   until it is entered: open the row's menu, **Enter its code**, and type it in. The code works for a day;
   **Send a new code** sends another.
3. Once connected, the address receives its topics. **Pause** stops it for a while, **Change topics** changes what it
   carries, and **Remove** stops it for good.

Everybody in the group sees where its notifications go, and whether an address is waiting for its code, sending or
paused.

To tell your team's own systems what happens — a tracker, an incident tool, a script — rather than its people, give the
group a [webhook](/docs/use/webhooks).
